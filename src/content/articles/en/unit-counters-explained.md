There is no unit in Age of Empires II without a weakness. "Countering" an enemy is not about memorising a chart — it is about understanding *why* one unit beats another. For the full interactive matrix, see the [Counters page](/en/counters/); this article explains the reasoning behind it.

## What makes a counter a counter

A unit only counters its target if it satisfies four things at once:

1. **It can actually kill the target.** An expensive unit beating a cheap one is not automatically a counter.
2. **Bonus damage.** Many counters carry a hidden attack bonus vs a unit class (check the exact numbers on [aoe2techtree.net](https://aoe2techtree.net)).
3. **Armour and pierce armour.** To counter ranged units you need high *pierce* armour — it is what lets Skirmishers shrug off arrows.
4. **Speed.** A hard counter is useless if the target simply runs away faster.

## The Feudal Age counter-square

The Feudal Age has one core cycle plus Men-at-Arms and towers:

- **Skirmisher beats Archer** — bonus damage plus 3 pierce armour reduces the archer's hit to 1.
- **Scout Cavalry beats Skirmisher** — 2 pierce armour means the skirm only deals 1, and the scout is fast enough to chase.
- **Spearman beats Scout Cavalry** — a large anti-cavalry bonus. Spears are the hardest counter in Feudal, but they are slow and melt to anything else.
- **Archer beats Spearman** — spears have low pierce armour and no answer to ranged fire.

Two units sit outside the square. **Men-at-Arms** beat most Feudal units head-on and are perfectly safe under a tower; only hit-and-run archers trouble them. **Eagle Scouts** (mesoamerican civs) have no clean Feudal counter.

## The Castle Age: compositions, not single units

In Castle Age a counter must also arrive *in time* and survive *micro*. The **Knight** is the dominant unit — high HP, attack, armour, and speed — at roughly twice the cost of anything before it. If you reach Castle Age first, a handful of Knights will wipe an unprepared Feudal army, so if you are behind in age, rushing up is the priority.

The most common answer is the **Crossbowman** — a cheap upgrade from your Feudal archers. The Xbow + Bodkin power spike cannot be contested by any Feudal unit, which is exactly why archer play is so popular. Against Knights you instead want **Pikemen** (cheap anti-cavalry, but they need numbers and support) or **Camel Riders** (mobile and bonus vs cavalry — the cleanest mobile anti-Knight). Massed archers in turn fold to a single **Mangonel**, unless their owner micros them apart.

The lesson: countering is as much about keeping your economy efficient while you transition as it is about picking the "right" unit.
