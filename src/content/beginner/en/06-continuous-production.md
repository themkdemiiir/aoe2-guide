Just as your Town Center should never idle a villager, your military buildings should never idle a unit. In a 1v1, the player who keeps every **Barracks, Archery Range, and Stable** producing non-stop ends up with more units on the field — and more units means more map control, more map control means a bigger economy, and the lead snowballs.

## Never idle a production building

Once you commit to a unit, queue it forever. An idle Range for 60 seconds is one missing archer in the fight that decides who controls the gold. The discipline is the same as villager production: **keep the queue full, keep the resources flowing.**

The catch is that constant production has a cost. If your economy can't feed the queue, the building idles anyway. So the real skill is balancing your villager distribution against how fast a building drains resources.

## How many villagers to feed a building

A rough beginner rule for Feudal archers:

| You want | Villagers needed |
|---|---|
| One Range of archers, non-stop | **~3.5 on gold** (use **4**) |
| Plus the wood for the archers | a few on wood |

Archers cost wood and gold, so you split villagers across both. The **~3.5 on gold** figure is the headline number: round it up to 4 so you run with a small surplus rather than starving the queue. If gold piles up, you have too many miners; if the building idles waiting for gold, you have too few.

Online **"villagers required" calculators** let you plug in a unit and a number of buildings and tell you exactly how many gatherers to put on each resource. Use one while learning — it builds the intuition fast.

## Lanchester's Square Law: why a small lead snowballs

Here is the math that makes continuous production so decisive. In an all-vs-all fight where every unit can hit any enemy, **fighting strength scales with the square of the number of units**, not just the count. This is **Lanchester's Square Law.**

What it means in practice: a slightly bigger army doesn't win by a slim margin — it wins with units to spare.

- **10 vs 11** archers: the side of 11 wins with roughly **3 survivors**, not 1.
- Those 3 survivors roll into the next fight. Now it's **10 vs 13**, and the bigger side comes out with about **7 survivors**.

Each engagement widens the gap. A two-unit edge becomes a three-unit win becomes a seven-unit win. This is exactly why the small things matter so much:

- **Idling 2–3 units** by letting a building sit empty isn't a rounding error — it can flip a fight.
- **Out-producing** your opponent by even one unit per cycle compounds into a crushing advantage over a few minutes.

## The takeaway

Continuous production and Lanchester's Law are two sides of one idea: in this game, **numbers compound.** Keep every military building working, feed the queue with the right villager balance (start with ~4 on gold per Range), and never throw away units for free. A lead that looks tiny on paper becomes a won game on the battlefield.
