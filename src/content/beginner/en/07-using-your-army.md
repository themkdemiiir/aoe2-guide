Once you have units on the field, winning fights is rarely about picking the perfect counter. It is about **numbers, positioning, and refusing to fight battles you cannot win**. Most beginner losses come from throwing armies into bad fights, not from choosing the wrong unit.

## Numbers beat counters

A "counter" only matters if you have a fair fight. With a big enough lead, raw numbers overwhelm the theory: **20 knights will crush 10 pikemen**, even though pikes are the textbook answer to cavalry. The counter relationship assumes roughly equal cost on the field. If you are far ahead, lean into it and trade your advantage in mass before the opponent stabilizes.

This is why economy and continuous production matter so much. The army that arrives bigger usually wins, and the gap **compounds**: every unit that survives a fight goes on to kill more in the next one.

## Never fight a losing fight

The most valuable skill is knowing when to **walk away**. If a fight is lost, retreat and preserve your numbers. Units kept alive are far more useful than units traded into a wall of pikes or archers.

Running is not cowardice, it is math. Even a **single militia** that survives can absorb a volley of arrows that would otherwise hit a more valuable unit. Every body that stays alive is one more body soaking damage and dealing it later.

## If you must fight outnumbered

Sometimes you have no choice. When that happens, do not fight on the enemy's terms:

- **Stack and micro backwards.** Pull your front units back as they take damage so fresh units rotate into contact. This spreads damage across your whole army instead of letting the front line melt.
- **Use high ground.** Units on a hill deal **+25% damage** and take **-25% damage**. That swing can flip a fight you would otherwise lose on flat ground.

## Forcing the enemy off a hill

If the **enemy** is the one sitting on high ground, do not feed your army into the hill. Instead, **poke their economy elsewhere** — raid villagers, threaten a second front, or snipe an undefended resource. They cannot hold the hill and defend their eco at the same time. Make them choose, and the moment they move, the high-ground advantage is gone.

## Power spikes are temporary

The instant you click up to **Castle Age**, you can play patiently behind walls and wait for your **Crossbow and Bodkin Armor** spike. Crossbowmen with Bodkin shred anything Feudal units can offer, and for a window you are simply stronger.

But a power spike is a **window, not a permanent state**. The moment your opponent reaches the same age, the advantage evaporates. So position to **abuse the spike before they age up** — push out, raid, or take map control while you own the timing. Sitting passively wastes the exact edge you spent resources to buy.

The thread connecting all of this: protect your army, fight on your terms, and cash in your advantages while they last.
